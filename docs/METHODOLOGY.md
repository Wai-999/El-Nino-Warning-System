# Methodology entry point

The current scientific and operational contract is [V2 Risk Methodology](RISK_METHODOLOGY.md). The full [source registry](DATA_SOURCES.md) records upstream licensing, resolution, timing, fallbacks and limitations. [Deployment](DEPLOYMENT.md) explains ingestion and release operations.

V1 methodology is preserved at commit `e0ddb952d2a57530597be920c071845d42ec236d`. V2 adds explicitly labeled, transparent application screening rules. These have not been validated against Myanmar impacts and are not government warning criteria. The V1 prohibition on invented operational values, forged official warnings and inferred confidence remains in effect.

## Impacts evidence and coverage repair (1 October 2026)

The impacts route now keeps current ENSO assessment, forecast weather, recent ERA5 reanalysis, historical relationships and scenario guidance explicitly separate. ERA5 is a model/observation synthesis, not station observations; no forecast anomaly is calculated. The historical FAO case concerns July 2015–March 2016, not current conditions or an event-attribution study. NOAA's general teleconnection summary does not specify composite years or comparable graded confidence, which the page discloses. No confidence score is assigned.

`src/data/impactEvidence.ts` records bilingual claims, geography, relationship, direction, confidence limitations, source IDs, publication metadata and verification dates. `src/risk/impacts.ts` defines sector driver coverage outside UI: health uses heat/rain, agriculture heat/rain/dryness, water water/rain, energy heat/water/wind. An elevated known driver may be shown even with incomplete coverage, but incomplete evidence must not yield an overall Normal label. This improves missing-data interpretation; all existing numeric hazard thresholds are unchanged. National counts explicitly distinguish complete, partial and unavailable coverage and do not count actual impacts.

Weather aging is 12–18 hours since either retrieval or modeled valid time, followed by stale at 18 hours (or earlier window expiry). ERA5 ages at 8 days and expires at 10 days after its last completed MMT day. These are application freshness policies, not confidence estimates. Retrieval/build times do not reset the actual forecast or observation period. Missing, stale, failed and cached inputs have distinct visible explanations.

Validation now checks rain percentages, seven-day sums and trailing dry-day counts against stored daily values, allowing the known 0.01 mm rounding interval around thresholds. Duplicate/unordered/invalid/incomplete NOAA index months are rejected. Client loading rejects future retrieval timestamps and incomplete observation windows, using only validated dated cache on failure. A corrupt operational snapshot is rejected as a unit; static guidance, history and the independent NOAA bulletin remain accessible.

Filters are stored in `#/impacts?sector=...&region=...&evidence=...`. The separate document query (including `?release=2.0.0`) is preserved. Regional summary uses all sector drivers; evidence filters change the detailed datasets shown. Sector scenarios and official ENSO context remain labeled context. Historical sources and thresholds are disclosed rather than inferred from an ENSO state.

## 2.1 regional intelligence contract (2 October 2026)

`src/risk/intelligence.ts` is the common coverage and regional-screening model used by Summary, Warnings, region details and the map's overall layer. All 15 canonical units always exist in unfiltered warnings. Canonical aliases resolve deterministically or throw; MIMU's Bago/Shan subdivisions have a separate geometry-only mapping and cannot silently widen an alert.

Overall platform concern is the maximum of independent heat, heavy rain, precipitation deficit and wind screening levels. An elevated known signal remains visible with a partial-evidence label. Low platform concern requires all four inputs to be assessed; all-known-low plus any missing input is insufficient data. Underlying categories remain `normal/advisory/watch/warning/severe`; the regional matrix translates these into Low platform risk / Attention / Elevated / High / Very high to distinguish them from official warnings. Existing individual signal cards retain the original threshold category names and prominent SYSTEM RISK SIGNAL label. Missing/stale never equals zero. Rainfall-deficit screening is unknown when its percentage is null or its climatological 30-day total is below 30 mm; this is a denominator/applicability gap, not normal dryness. Percent-of-normal is `100 + percentage departure`, displayed only when the departure is valid; it is neither a percentile nor a probability.

All active, validated Myanmar official advisories take precedence, including advisory-level bulletins. Source hostname, country `MM`, registry ID `dmh`, source-described location, canonical ID, translations and validity interval are mandatory. WMO/FAO/WHO/ASMC context does not pass the Myanmar official-warning schema. Incomplete official coverage stays unavailable even with no active alerts. A complete, freshly checked feed may say “No active official warning found”; that is not low platform risk. Prior API clients/caches containing unsupported official metadata fail closed.

Coverage is the count of current/aging evidence cells across 15 regions × 8 categories = 120. The categories are temperature reanalysis, precipitation reanalysis, short-range forecast, drought assessment, flood assessment, agriculture context, health guidance and official-advisory coverage. Monitoring has 90 possible cells; national/general context has 30. Their counts are shown separately as well as combined. With current forecast/ERA5 plus current FAO/WHO context, coverage is 75/120 (45/90 monitoring, 30/30 context). No locality-specific crop stage, disease incidence, drought, river level or flood forecast is claimed by those context cells.

The source panel joins registry metadata to the original provider periods, retrieval times and failure logs. Source freshness and last-fetch failure are independent: a failed refresh can retain still-valid data. Forecast is current under 12 h, aging 12–18 h, then stale or at validity end; ERA5 is aging at 8 days, stale at 10 days after its last completed Myanmar day. NOAA assessment ages after 21 days and expires on the source next-update date. Monthly Niño 3.4 uses the latest monthly period (aging at 62 days, stale at 93 days from month start). ASMC is manually reviewed, aging at 35 days, stale at 62 or validity expiry; FAO crop context ages at 45 and expires at 90 days from publication; WHO guidance ages at 90 and expires at 180 days from editorial review. Scheduled weather retrieval never changes these context dates. All are operational display policies, not statistical confidence.

`public/data/archive.json` contains up to 124 distinct validated screening summaries, including methods, source IDs, units, baseline, forecast/reanalysis periods, all 15 regional hazard levels, coverage and official IDs. A refresh with an identical summary does not manufacture another change. Validated official bulletins are deduplicated and retained until 365 days after validity end; full source snapshots remain in Git. Corrupt input aborts archive writing; writes use atomic rename. The client also validates archive structure and future timestamps before caching and uses only validated dated cache on failure. The new baseline starts in this release, not retroactively. At the regular four-check cadence, 124 entries cover roughly a month; manual releases can shorten it.

“What changed?” compares the two latest compatible stored summaries. Unknown→known is data restored; known→unknown is data lost, never improved risk. Higher/lower known thresholds are changes in screening states. Forecasts may have different valid windows, so the comparison is not an observed trend or causal conclusion. A single stored summary explicitly lacks a comparison. Coverage and static context never adjust hazard severity.

Records contain source-backed reported impacts with event-period precision, publication/review dates, explicit geographic scope, ENSO attribution status and limitations. Region filters include only explicitly named areas. A national report is not expanded across every region. Dates used to filter month-precision events are administrative endpoints and displayed only to month precision. Unsupported historical heat, drought, health and fire/haze inventories are disclosed as gaps. Retained historical bulletins are explicitly expired, not active alerts.

See [research decisions](../WARNING_SYSTEM_RESEARCH.md), [release audit](../V2_0_1_UPGRADE_AUDIT.md) and [source registry](DATA_SOURCES.md). Numeric meteorological thresholds remain unchanged; no SPI/SPEI, flood probability, crop-loss or disease-outbreak score was introduced.

The service worker precaches with HTTP-cache reload semantics so an older HTML document cannot survive into a new release; its fingerprint also includes cache-policy source. It activates a completed new shell immediately and keeps the prior hashed shell for old open tabs’ lazy chunks. Navigation uses the current shell; only the current and one prior shell are retained. Current data and archive JSON remain network-first with explicit dated fallback. The `release.json` and visible footer identify the actual installed build independently of the URL query.

## 2.2 location, health and KPI contract (4 October 2026)

Impacts defaults to country `MM`, then the 15 canonical units. Invalid URL locations fall back to the country, never an arbitrary region. Full current ENSO is owned by Summary; historical evidence by Records; explanatory mechanism and media by Learn; full coverage matrix by Data. Links retain region/sector/evidence selection. Country rankings expose up to five elevated available sector screens with complete/partial/unavailable counts. These are neither affected-region counts nor a damage ranking. Unsupported sectors have explicit location-evidence gaps. No numerical hazard thresholds changed.

`nationalImpactMetrics` computes min/max across available regional estimates for next-24-hour maximum temperature and rainfall, recent 30-day temperature anomaly and trailing dry days. This is not a national average: no cross-region population/area weights are asserted. Current/aging data contribute; stale/unavailable inputs yield null ranges, never zero. Metric periods, source, unit, baseline/applicability, retrieval and decision purpose are visible. Dry-day counts are capped at 30 and therefore 30 means at least 30; percentage departure and count are not drought probabilities. Regional dated stale values remain inspectable but excluded from screening.

`decisionKpis` centralizes the four existing real decision tiles. Highest concern uses known independent hazards, preserving the partial-evidence rule. Watch-region counts represent canonical regions with a verified official bulletin or watch-or-higher screening; incomplete meteorological input or official coverage prefixes a lower-bound sign. With no usable hazard evidence and no official alerts the count is unavailable, not zero. Official totals require complete current coverage to display zero; with partial coverage known active alerts form a lower bound. Known alerts preserve their issue/validity windows and source retrieval even when a complete-coverage timestamp is unavailable; watch-region provenance includes those contributing bulletins. Coverage remains 15 × 8 with monitoring and guidance separated. Each KPI exposes calculation, source, units, original contributing periods, latest contributing retrieval/review and why it matters. Coverage is explicitly evaluated at the current assessment time. No fake KPI was found or invented; existing tiles gained provenance and safer missing-value wording.

The general Health knowledge contract is in [Health methodology](../HEALTH_GUIDANCE_METHODOLOGY.md). Clinical guidance and manually reviewed qualitative surveillance are separate. Surveillance freshness is publication-based (30/60-day thresholds), not observation completeness. The 90/180-day guidance review policy keeps emergency information accessible while displaying aging. Neither supplies regional disease incidence or modifies weather concern.

Monthly Niño 3.4 freshness now uses the same 62/93-day policy in Summary and the source registry (removing the inconsistent 100-day display cutoff). It remains SST anomaly, not RONI or ONI. RONI is linked to the original official rolling three-month index; no unvalidated parser was added.

Archive JSON is requested by the application when Summary/Records need it or those pages are refreshed. Maps remain lazy. Health and media are lazy route chunks; video thumbnails require opt-in, use a narrowly allowed `i.ytimg.com` image origin and pause in Low Data mode. No third-party player, autoplay, script or video fetch is embedded. The offline worker still precaches local assets for full offline use; this intentionally differs from initial application execution/lazy rendering. Existing atomic ingestion and dated fallback semantics are unchanged.
