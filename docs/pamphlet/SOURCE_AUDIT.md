## 1. How the edition was read

- **What the file is.** A 2-page A4-landscape PDF exported by _PowerPoint → Print → Save as PDF_ (Quartz), scaled to
  96.6 % with ~5 mm white margins. Outside sheet = back cover | front cover; inside sheet = panel 2 | panel 3; the fold is at
  the page centre. It is a print of an edited copy of the earlier editable master: new teal footer band, a second
  logo (GiHman Humanitarian Aid Initiative Committee – Tanintharyi), publisher credits, an edited heat-health card, a new
  “note” chip and other text edits. The `.pptx` it was printed from was **not** in the shared folder, so the PDF is the only
  editable source available here (it is _not_ a fully editable file despite its name).
- **Burmese text.** Copy-paste from this PDF is unreliable (glyph-based subsets, visual order). The Unicode text used on the
  website was reconstructed from 220–600 dpi renders and the earlier copy deck, then checked **glyph by glyph** against the
  PDF's embedded Noto Sans Myanmar subsets (HarfBuzz shaping vs. the PDF glyph stream, `verify_transcription.py`):
  **80 text blocks checked, 0 mismatches**; the only PDF text not covered is the list-bullet glyph (■) and
  the `[OFFICIAL_SOURCE_QR]` label. On the QR edition the same check gives 80 blocks / 1 mismatch — the line deliberately
  replaced by the QR caption (see CHANGELOG).
- **Sources.** Every cited source was fetched again; the exact sentences relied on are machine-checked in the saved text
  (`check_sources.py`, results in `source_evidence.json`). An HTTP 200 or a matching phrase does **not** by itself make a
  citation valid — interpretation was reviewed by hand and is summarised here.

## 3. Findings that need an editorial decision (not applied to print)

1. **C18 — needs correction (panel 3, outlook box, bullet 3).** Printed: “အင်အားကြီးလာခြင်းက ဒေသတစ်ခုချင်း၏ ထိခိုက်မှုကို
   တိုက်ရိုက် မသက်ရောက်ပါ (WMO)။” (“the increase in strength does not directly affect each region's impacts”). WMO actually
   says: _“the strength of an El Niño event alone does not determine how severe its impacts will be in any individual country
   or region”_ (S03), and NOAA says that with an event of this magnitude _“the chances of experiencing impacts consistent with
   El Niño are larger, though not guaranteed”_ (S01). The printed wording implies strength has no direct effect, which neither
   agency says. **Proposed:** “အင်အားကြီးမှု တစ်ခုတည်းက ဒေသတစ်ခုချင်း၏ ထိခိုက်မှု ပြင်းထန်ပုံကို မဆုံးဖြတ်ပါ (WMO)။”
2. **C16 — accurate but dated (panel 3, bullet 2).** “WMO (စက်တင်ဘာ ၂၀၂၆) … ၁၀၀% နီးပါး … နှစ်ကုန်ပိုင်း အထွတ်အထိပ်” matches
   WMO's 3 Sep 2026 update (S02/S03). WMO restated it on **8 Oct 2026** with a forecast **December** peak (S20, S21) — the
   same date the cover footer gives for “WMO, NOAA” data. **Proposed:** “WMO (၈ အောက်တိုဘာ ၂၀၂၆) — ၂၀၂၇ ဖေဖော်ဝါရီအထိ ဆက်ရှိနိုင်ခြေ ၁၀၀% နီးပါးရှိပြီး ၂၀၂၆ ဒီဇင်ဘာတွင် အထွတ်အထိပ် ရောက်မည်ဟု ခန့်မှန်းသည်။”
3. **Typo — panel 2 diagram, third box:** printed “… မိုးခေါင်နိုင်ခြင်’။” (stray apostrophe, missing visarga, confirmed in
   the glyph stream). **Proposed:** “မြန်မာ — မိုးမမှန်ခြင်း၊ ပိုပူ၊ မိုးခေါင်နိုင်ခြင်း။”
4. **Typo — panel 4:** “အပြည့်အစုံကို QR တွင်ဖတ်ရှူနိုင်ပါသည်။” (ရှူ for ရှု). This line was replaced in the QR edition by the
   required caption, so no further action is needed unless the line is restored.
5. **Publication date incomplete (cover):** “ထုတ်ဝေသည့်ရက် — ____ ရက် အောက်တိုဘာလ ၂၀၂၆ခုနှစ်။” — the day is blank. Fill it in

6. **Optional clarity — panel 3 chip:** “မှတ်ချက် — တိကျစွာတိုင်းတာမှု မဟုတ်” (“not a precise measurement”) is true but weaker

## 4. Specific checks requested in the brief

- **2026–27 WMO/NOAA statements.** NOAA CPC discussion of 8 Oct 2026 (archive URL, S01): El Niño Advisory; “strong-to-very
  strong El Niño likely through January-March 2027 (remaining greater than an 83% chance)”; “historic event” chances 54 % SON,
  83 % OND, 70 % NDJ. These are **probabilities**; the leaflet says “ခန့်မှန်းချက်သာ” (forecast only) and does not use “super”.
  WMO: near-100 % persistence through Feb 2027 (S02/S03, S20); peak towards end-2026 / December (S02, S20, S21).
- **Historic claims.** FAO GIEWS Feb 1998 (S08) names Indonesia, China, the Philippines and Thailand — exactly the four in the
  leaflet. OCHA 16 Sep 2015 (S09): “temporarily displacing over 1.6 million people” (OCHA's later figure ≈1.7 million, S10);
  the leaflet does **not** attribute the floods to El Niño. World Bank 2019 report (original PDF, S11): “during the 2016 El
  Niño, 1,700 villages experienced water shortages”. AFP via Myanmar Now (S12): 48.2 °C at Chauk on Sunday 28 Apr 2024, the
  highest April temperature since records began 56 years ago; NOAA (S13) shows the event was waning — the leaflet adds that
  El Niño was not the only cause.
- **Heat first aid.** Signs (dizziness, headache, nausea/vomiting, cramps, heavy sweating or hot dry skin, fast heartbeat),
  steps (cool shade, loosen clothing, wet cloth and fanning, small sips only if conscious and able to swallow) and red flags
  (confusion, collapse/unconsciousness, seizure, suspected heatstroke → emergency care, do not wait) all appear in S16, S17
  and S22; WHO (S15) confirms heatstroke is a medical emergency and the at-risk groups. No medicines are mentioned.
