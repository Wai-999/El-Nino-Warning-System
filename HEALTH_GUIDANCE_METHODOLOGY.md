# Health guidance methodology · 2.2.0

Reviewed 4 October 2026. This is public preparedness and first aid, not diagnosis, a clinical decision support device or a disease forecasting model. The Health page is the primary home for symptoms and care thresholds. Independent clinical and native Burmese review have not been completed; this is a disclosed limitation, not a claimed approval.

## Evidence model

`src/data/healthGuidance.ts` defines nine bilingual records: dehydration, heat cramps, heat exhaustion, heatstroke, acute watery diarrhoea/sanitation, dengue, malaria, smoke/haze irritation and food safety. Each contains exposure pathway, susceptible groups, early signs, immediate low-risk action, medical-care threshold, emergency signs, prevention and authoritative source IDs. All clinical fields in a record use its listed clinical references; prevention may additionally use WHO prevention material. Links and publication/review dates are visible with each topic. No individual diagnosis, dose, antibiotic prescription, antimalarial regimen or infection likelihood is supplied.

The site does not transplant US 911 or UK 999/111 numbers. It directs people to reachable local emergency care. Heatstroke wording never tells a person with emergency signs to wait for a thermometer, a cooling trial or a weather alert. Universal fluid-volume targets are omitted because individual restrictions vary. ORS instructions defer to the actual packet and safe water; no home recipe is invented. Fever after mosquito exposure requires clinical assessment, not a weather-based diagnosis.

## Burmese resources and translation

Official WHO Myanmar materials are linked for [heat](https://www.who.int/myanmar/emergencies/protect-yourself-from-heat--heatwave), [monsoon/floods](https://www.who.int/myanmar/emergencies/safety-during-monsoons-and-floods), [acute watery diarrhoea](https://www.who.int/india/multimedia/item/acute-watery-diarrhoea-q-a-%28myanmar%29) (18 July 2024, WHO Myanmar team, despite the site's `/india/` path), and [food safety](https://www.who.int/southeastasia/multimedia/item/5-keys-to-safer-food-poster-%28myanmar%29) (10 August 2023). English and Burmese site copy are editorial summaries, not certified translations. The Learn glossary preserves English scientific terms where needed: reanalysis, anomaly, RONI, exposure/vulnerability, heatstroke and ORS.

Myanmar MOH public access was unsuccessful with the research browser. NUG MOH's public guideline catalogue was evaluated as a separately identified publisher; specialist dengue treatment protocols were not repackaged as lay medication instructions. Neither absence of a connected feed nor access failure means an authority has issued no advice.

## Location and surveillance separation

Default location is country `MM`; the selector supports only the 15 canonical administrative units. Heat/rain inputs come from the existing independently validated forecast screens with dates. No weather input modifies the written care threshold or hides any topic. Weather data loss leaves all first aid available offline.

The [WHO / Health Cluster August 2026 bulletin](https://myanmar.un.org/en/321949-myanmar-health-cluster-bulletin-august-2026), published 31 August and reviewed 4 October, is shown under **DISEASE SURVEILLANCE · DATED REPORT**. It is qualitative reported evidence, not a complete comparable current incidence series. Sittwe maps to Rakhine and Shwebo to Sagaing only for finding the reported locality; visible text explicitly prevents expansion to every township. Missing regions have no report in this reviewed source, not zero cases. Neither the report nor its counts enter hazard severity or the coverage model's health-guidance cells.

Freshness is a display policy: surveillance current through 30 days from publication, aging through 60, stale afterward; future publications are unavailable. General health guidance ages after 90 days from editorial review and is stale after 180; source publication dates remain separate. Expired review does not remove emergency guidance. No weather refresh resets either date. Next manual research target: 4 November 2026, not an automation created by this release.

## Validation limits

Unit checks cover required pathways, source references, both languages, emergency immediacy, safe swallowing, severe dengue timing, ORS packet directions, early malaria testing and absence of doses/disease prediction. Browser checks exercise every topic with failed data, location selection, independent surveillance, source links, phone/tablet layouts and WCAG automated rules. These checks support implementation quality; they are not independent clinical validation or certification of Burmese terminology.
